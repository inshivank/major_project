# Fever in adults

Source: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/fever-in-adults/

A fever is a body temperature of 38°C (100.4°F) or higher. It is usually a sign that the body is fighting an infection, most often a viral illness such as a cold or flu. Fever itself is not an illness and is rarely dangerous in otherwise healthy adults.

Common causes include respiratory infections, urinary tract infections, gastroenteritis, and skin infections. Less common causes include inflammatory conditions, reactions to medicines, and, rarely, serious infections such as pneumonia, meningitis, or sepsis.

Most fevers settle within a few days with rest, plenty of fluids, and light clothing. Over-the-counter medicines such as paracetamol or ibuprofen can lower temperature and ease discomfort, but they do not treat the underlying cause. Read the label carefully and do not exceed the stated dose.

Seek urgent medical help if a fever is accompanied by a stiff neck, a rash that does not fade when pressed, confusion, difficulty breathing, severe headache, repeated vomiting, a seizure, or a fever above 40°C that does not respond to treatment. Seek urgent help if the person is immunosuppressed, pregnant, has recently returned from an area where malaria is common, or has a long-term condition that affects the immune system.

People who are very young, very old, pregnant, or receiving chemotherapy may become seriously unwell quickly and should be assessed earlier rather than later. A persistent fever lasting more than five days, or a fever that keeps returning, should be reviewed by a clinician to identify the cause.
