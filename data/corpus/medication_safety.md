# Using medicines safely

Source: https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-you-drugs/think-it-through-questions-ask-about-your-medicines

Medicines work best and are safest when used exactly as prescribed or as directed on the label. Always read the label and the patient information leaflet, and follow the dose, timing, and route instructions. Do not change a dose or stop a medicine without discussing it with a clinician or pharmacist.

Keep an up-to-date list of all medicines, including over-the-counter products, vitamins, herbal remedies, and supplements. This list helps clinicians check for interactions and duplicate ingredients. Many combination cold and pain products contain the same active ingredient, so taking several at once can lead to accidental overdose, particularly with paracetamol.

Antibiotics only treat bacterial infections. They do not help colds, flu, or most sore throats. Taking them unnecessarily causes side effects and contributes to antibiotic resistance. If prescribed an antibiotic, complete the course as directed unless a clinician tells you to stop.

Store medicines as directed, out of reach of children, and check expiry dates. Some medicines need refrigeration or protection from light. Bring unwanted medicines to a pharmacy for safe disposal rather than flushing them or throwing them in household waste.

Seek urgent help for signs of a severe allergic reaction such as swelling of the face or throat, difficulty breathing, or a widespread rash with faintness. Other warning signs include severe stomach pain, blood in vomit or stool, unusual bruising or bleeding, yellowing of the eyes, and confusion after starting a new medicine. Pregnant or breastfeeding women should check with a pharmacist before using any new medicine.
