# High blood pressure (hypertension)

Source: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/hypertension

Blood pressure is the force of blood pushing against artery walls. It is recorded as two numbers: systolic pressure (when the heart contracts) over diastolic pressure (when the heart relaxes). Hypertension is generally defined as readings of 140/90 mmHg or higher on repeated measurement, although thresholds vary by guideline and by setting.

Hypertension usually causes no symptoms. Headaches, nosebleeds, and dizziness are not reliable signs, and many people only discover the condition through a routine check. Because it silently damages organs, it is often called the silent killer.

Risk factors include older age, family history, excess body weight, high salt intake, low physical activity, alcohol use, smoking, and diabetes. Secondary causes such as kidney disease or hormonal disorders are less common but should be considered in younger people or when blood pressure is difficult to control.

Treatment starts with lifestyle change: reducing salt, eating more fruit and vegetables, maintaining a healthy weight, exercising regularly, limiting alcohol, and stopping smoking. Most people also need medication, often more than one type. Treatment is long term, and stopping medicines without advice allows blood pressure to rise again.

Uncontrolled hypertension raises the risk of heart attack, stroke, heart failure, kidney disease, and vision problems. Regular home monitoring and review of medicines help keep readings in range.

A reading of 180/120 mmHg or higher with chest pain, breathlessness, severe headache, visual disturbance, or weakness on one side of the body is a medical emergency. Call emergency services rather than waiting for a routine appointment.
