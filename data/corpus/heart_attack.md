# Heart attack warning signs

Source: https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/heart-attack/warning-signs-of-a-heart-attack

A heart attack happens when blood flow to part of the heart muscle is blocked, usually by a clot in a coronary artery. The longer the blockage lasts, the more muscle is damaged, so recognising symptoms and getting help quickly is critical. Every minute of delay costs heart muscle.

The most common symptom is chest discomfort in the centre or left side of the chest that lasts more than a few minutes or comes and goes. It may feel like pressure, squeezing, fullness, or pain. It can spread to the left arm, both arms, neck, jaw, shoulder, or back. Other symptoms include shortness of breath, cold sweat, nausea or vomiting, light-headedness, and unusual tiredness.

Women, older adults, and people with diabetes may have less typical symptoms, such as breathlessness, nausea, jaw or back pain, or extreme fatigue without prominent chest pain. Symptoms can start slowly or appear while resting.

Call emergency services immediately for suspected heart attack. Do not drive yourself to hospital and do not wait to see whether symptoms pass. While waiting for help, sit or lie down, stay calm, and follow dispatcher instructions. If advised and not allergic, chewing aspirin may be recommended by emergency staff.

Do not dismiss symptoms as indigestion or muscle strain when they are new, severe, or accompanied by sweating and breathlessness. Rapid treatment with clot-dissolving medicine or a procedure to reopen the artery saves lives and limits damage. After a heart attack, cardiac rehabilitation and risk-factor control are important parts of recovery.
