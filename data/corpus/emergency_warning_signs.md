# When to seek emergency care

Source: https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/urgent-and-emergency-care-services/when-to-call-999/

Some symptoms need immediate assessment by emergency services rather than a routine appointment or a call to a helpline. Recognising these situations and acting without delay can be life-saving.

Call your local emergency number for chest pain or pressure that is new, severe, or lasting more than a few minutes; sudden weakness or numbness in the face, arm, or leg, especially on one side; sudden confusion or difficulty speaking; sudden loss of vision; difficulty breathing or choking; severe bleeding that does not stop with pressure; a seizure that does not stop or a first-ever seizure; loss of consciousness; a severe allergic reaction with swelling of the face or throat; and thoughts of harming yourself or others.

Emergency care is also needed after a serious injury, a head injury with vomiting or drowsiness, a suspected overdose, a severe burn, or a fever with a stiff neck, rash that does not fade under pressure, or confusion. Pregnant women should seek urgent help for heavy bleeding, severe abdominal pain, severe headache with visual changes, or reduced fetal movement.

While waiting for an ambulance, stay with the person, keep them comfortable, and follow instructions from the dispatcher. Do not give food or drink to someone who is drowsy or has difficulty swallowing. Do not leave a person with chest pain alone.

If the situation is urgent but not immediately life-threatening, an urgent care service or same-day appointment may be appropriate. If you are unsure, call the emergency number or a nurse advice line; it is safer to be assessed promptly than to wait. This information is general guidance and does not replace an emergency dispatcher's assessment.
