# When to see a doctor about a symptom

Source: https://www.nhs.uk/symptom-checker/

Many symptoms improve on their own, but some need timely assessment. A useful approach is to consider how severe the symptom is, how long it has lasted, whether it is getting worse, and whether it occurs with other warning signs.

Book a routine appointment for symptoms that persist for more than a few weeks, keep returning, or interfere with daily life. Examples include persistent cough, unexplained weight loss, changes in bowel habit, a new lump, unusual bleeding, long-standing fatigue, and changes in a mole. Early assessment of possible cancer symptoms improves outcomes.

Seek same-day urgent advice for fever with confusion, worsening breathlessness, severe abdominal pain, a new rash with fever, vomiting that prevents fluids, or a wound that becomes red, hot, and painful. Seek emergency care for chest pain, stroke symptoms, severe bleeding, breathing difficulty, or loss of consciousness.

Before an appointment, note when symptoms started, what makes them better or worse, all medicines and allergies, and any recent travel or illness. Photographs of a rash or swelling can be helpful. Bring a list of questions.

Do not use online information, including AI assistants, to diagnose a symptom or to decide against seeking care. General guidance can help a patient prepare for a consultation but cannot replace examination, tests, and clinical judgement. For anything sudden, severe, or rapidly worsening, contact emergency services without delay.
